State of Nature Activity

Before we begin

How this works

01

You already wrote an Initial Settlement Plan. Keep it in front of you — every event will pressure-test it.

02

Each event presents a dispute with no clean answer. Decide as a group, then record the decision before moving on.

03

Decisions carry forward. If Event 2 creates a property rule, that rule is now part of your community when Event 4 hits.

04

At the end, you'll fill out a Final Settlement Plan — and answer the boundary question: did you build a state?

1

The Unpopular Work

After several days, most people are doing the jobs your settlement needs. But one person repeatedly refuses the hardest and least pleasant work.

“I never agreed that everyone has to work. If other people want to do those jobs, that’s their choice.”The person who refuses

They still eat the community’s food, use the tools, and sleep in the shared shelter. They are not violent and have not stolen anything.

Your challenge

Decide how your community will respond.

Can the group require this person to work? Can they lose access to shared resources? Or must everyone be allowed to decide for themselves?

Record your decision in the Challenge Log.
2

Food Is Running Low

The settlement’s first attempt at gathering and hunting has gone badly. You now have much less food than expected.

There is enough for everyone to survive if people accept smaller portions.

Late one evening, however, someone notices that several people have begun quietly hiding extra food for themselves. When confronted, they explain:

“I don’t know whether everyone else will follow the rationing plan. I’m not risking my life by trusting them.”One of the people hiding food

Nobody knows how many other people may be doing the same thing.

Your challenge

Decide what your community will do.

Think carefully about whether the main problem is selfishness, fear, distrust, scarcity, or something else.

Record your decision in the Challenge Log.
3

“This Land Is Mine”

One member of the settlement has spent weeks clearing an unused piece of land, improving the soil, and planting crops.

The crops are now beginning to grow.

“I did the work. This land and everything it produces belong to me.”The farmer

Another group member objects:

“The land was here before any of us arrived. You can’t just take part of it and make it yours.”Another group member

The farmer says that if they cannot keep a significant share of what they produce, they may stop doing the work.

Your challenge

Decide whether this person has gained a special claim to the land or its crops.

If so, what makes the claim legitimate? If not, why should the person continue doing the extra work?

Record your decision in the Challenge Log.
4

Everyone Thinks They Are Right

Two people get into a serious dispute over a missing tool.

One claims the other took it without permission. The accused person says the tool had been abandoned and that anyone was free to use it.

Both are completely convinced that they are right.

Their friends begin taking sides. Each side insists that the other side is lying or twisting the facts. The disagreement is becoming angry, but nobody has become violent.

Your challenge

Decide who should judge the dispute and whether that decision must be accepted.

Can the people involved judge their own case? Should the whole community vote? Should a neutral person decide? What gives anyone the authority to make the final judgment?

Record your decision in the Challenge Log.
5

The Settlement Begins to Divide

Several months have passed. Some people have become especially successful.

A few are better hunters. Others control highly valued skills or productive land. Some people have accumulated more useful goods than others.

People also begin caring more about who is respected, who is influential, and who has more than everyone else.

Arguments that once concerned survival now include comments such as:

“Why should they have more than us?”One member
“I earned what I have.”Another member
“People listen to her because she thinks she’s better than everyone.”Another member
“Why should I keep helping people who contribute less?”Another member

Two loose factions are beginning to form.

Your challenge

Decide whether your community should change its rules because inequality, comparison, and status are beginning to affect how people treat one another.

Or are jealousy and resentment simply problems individuals need to control themselves?

Record your decision in the Challenge Log.
6

The Group Refuses the Group

A serious emergency develops. A dangerous storm is expected within two days, and the settlement needs everyone to help reinforce the shelter and move supplies.

After debate, your community makes a decision about what everyone must do.

Five people refuse.

“You can make whatever rules you want. We never gave you the right to control us.”The five people who refuse

They intend to keep their share of the food and tools but will not follow the group’s emergency plan.

Some members of the settlement now argue that rules are meaningless unless the community has the power to force people to obey them.

Others respond that the moment the group can force peaceful people to obey, the settlement has created exactly the kind of political power it originally did not have.

Your challenge

Decide what happens next.

May the community compel the five people to cooperate? If they continue to refuse, how far may the group go?

Then ask yourselves:
Have you now created a government? If so, what made it necessary?

Record your final decision in the Challenge Log.

Debrief · Part 2

Enforcement, legitimacy, and the boundary question

  • Enforcement — What happens when someone refuses a collective decision? Can the community use force — never, only to prevent immediate harm, for limited rules, or for all legitimate decisions?
  • Legitimacy — Why should someone who disagrees accept your community's decisions? Is consent required?
  • The boundary question — At what point, if any, did your community become a state? What feature of your system best supports that answer?
